# Transferring Capital Into and Out of the Vietnam International Financial Centre

## Summary

The transfer of capital into and out of the [[What Is The Vietnam International Financial Centre|Vietnam International Financial Centre]] (TTTC) is governed by a suite of decrees issued by the Vietnamese Government Office. These rules form part of the broader [[Legal Framework Of Tttc]] established to regulate financial flows, investor rights, and operational conditions within the centre. Key instruments include [[Decree 324 Financial Policies Of Tttc]], which addresses financial policies, and [[Decree 329 Banking And Foreign Exchange]], which covers banking and [[Foreign Exchange Rules In The Vietnam International Financial Centre|foreign exchange]] matters relevant to [[Capital Movement Rules In The Vietnam International Financial Centre|capital movement]]. Together, these decrees aim to facilitate [[How The Vietnam International Financial Centre Attracts Foreign Capital|foreign capital inflows]] while maintaining appropriate regulatory oversight.

## Key Points

- The decrees governing the TTTC were issued by VĂN PHÒNG CHÍNH PHỦ (the Government Office of Vietnam), indicating they carry the authority of the central government. [1][2][3][4][5][6]
- [[Decree 324 Financial Policies Of Tttc]] is the primary instrument addressing financial policies within the TTTC, which would include matters related to capital transfer. [2]
- [[Decree 323 Establishment Of Tttc]] establishes the legal basis for the TTTC itself, forming the foundational framework within which capital transfer rules operate.
- [[Decree 326 Land And Environment In Tttc]] governs land and environmental policy within the TTTC, which may intersect with asset-based capital considerations.
- [[Decree 325 Labor And Social Security In Tttc]] covers labour and social security, which may relate to the transfer of income and remuneration for workers operating within the centre.
- [[Decree 327 Immigration Policy]] addresses entry and exit policies, which may be relevant to the movement of foreign investors and their associated capital activities.
- [[Decree 328 International Arbitration Center]] establishes the international arbitration centre, providing [[Dispute Resolution Mechanisms]] relevant to disputes arising from capital transfers. [1]
- [[Decree 329 Banking And Foreign Exchange]] contains specific provisions governing [[Ngoai Hoi In The Context Of Tttc|foreign exchange management]] (quản lý ngoại hối) for borrowing and lending activities by members of the TTTC. [5]
- [[Decree 330 Commodities Exchange In Tttc]] governs the establishment and operation of the commodities exchange within the TTTC, which involves pre-agreed settlement arrangements relevant to capital flows. [6]

## Borrowing and Lending Across Borders

[[Decree 329 Banking And Foreign Exchange]] (Section 4, Articles 87–89) sets out the rules for [[Foreign Exchange Rules In The Vietnam International Financial Centre|foreign exchange management]] applicable to borrowing and lending by TTTC members:

- **Borrowing from offshore individuals and organisations:** Members (thành viên) are permitted to borrow foreign currency from individuals and organisations outside Vietnamese territory, subject to mandatory information declaration and compliance with reporting obligations. [5]
- **Lending abroad — 100% foreign-owned members:** Members whose charter capital is 100% owned by foreign investors are permitted to lend to individuals and organisations outside Vietnamese territory, subject to information declaration and reporting requirements. [5]
- **Lending abroad — non-100% foreign-owned members:** Members whose charter capital is not 100% foreign-owned must comply with the conditions for offshore lending set out in Article 89 of [[Decree 329 Banking And Foreign Exchange]], in addition to information declaration and reporting obligations. [5]
- **Conditions for offshore lending by domestic banks:** A 100% domestically-owned commercial bank member may lend foreign currency to: (a) organisations established and operating outside Vietnamese territory in which a Vietnamese enterprise (non-member) or a Vietnamese member holds more than 50% of shares or contributed capital; and (b) organisations established and operating outside Vietnamese territory that are majority-owned (more than 50%) by such organisations. [5]
- **Conditions for offshore lending by enterprise members:** An enterprise member may lend to organisations established and operating outside Vietnamese territory in which the lending member or its parent company holds more than 50% of shares or contributed capital. [5]

These provisions create a tiered framework for [[International Capital Flows In The Vietnam International Financial Centre|cross-border capital flows]], with more liberal rules for fully foreign-owned entities and more restrictive conditions for domestically-owned or mixed-ownership members.

## Banking Activities Relevant to Capital Transfer

[[Decree 329 Banking And Foreign Exchange]] also sets out a range of [[Banking Activities Allowed In The Vietnam International Financial Centre|banking activities]] permitted within the TTTC that are directly relevant to capital transfer:

- **Purchase and sale of Vietnamese Government bonds**, government-guaranteed bonds, and local government bonds in accordance with applicable bond legislation. [4]
- **Debt purchase and sale** (mua, bán nợ). [4]
- **Trust and entrusted activities** (ủy thác, nhận ủy thác) in banking operations, in accordance with the policies of the owner or parent bank and subject to the principles set out in Article 51 of the same decree. [4]
- **Advisory services** on banking operations and other business activities, within the scope of activities permitted under the licence issued by the city-level Managing Authority (Cơ quan điều hành). [4]
- **Cash management services, asset custody, and safe deposit box rental.** [4]

Member banks are also permitted to purchase and sell bonds issued by organisations outside Vietnamese territory, excluding bonds issued by Vietnamese credit institutions. [4]

## Arbitration for Capital Transfer Disputes

[[Decree 328 International Arbitration Center]] provides that the [[Arbitration In The Vietnam International Financial Centre|International Arbitration Centre]] (Trung tâm trọng tài quốc tế) has jurisdiction to resolve disputes arising from investment and business activities at the TTTC, in accordance with rules and regulations issued by the Centre itself, based on agreement between the parties. [1]

The following categories of disputes fall **outside** the jurisdiction of the International Arbitration Centre:

- Disputes involving administrative decisions or administrative acts of state management agencies or competent persons. [1]
- Labour disputes. [1]
- Disputes relating to the personal rights of individuals. [1]
- Other state management matters already resolved by a competent state management agency or a competent court in Vietnam. [1]

Courts may not refuse to hear applications to set aside arbitral awards or decisions of the Arbitral Tribunal where: (a) the waiver agreement falls within the categories of void agreements under Article 18 of the Law on Commercial Arbitration; or (b) the agreement was formed after one party had already filed an application to set aside the award or decision with a court. [1]

This arbitration framework is directly relevant to the resolution of disputes arising from capital transfers, including [[Cross Border Ma In The Vietnam International Financial Centre|cross-border transactions]] and [[Foreign Investment Rules In Tttc|foreign investment]] arrangements. See also [[How Disputes Are Handled In The Vietnam International Financial Centre]] and [[Dispute Resolution In The Vietnam International Financial Centre]].

## Winding Up of Insurance Enterprises and Debt Priority

[[Decree 324 Financial Policies Of Tttc]] provides that upon dissolution of an insurance enterprise, reinsurance enterprise, or insurance brokerage enterprise operating within the TTTC, outstanding debts are to be settled in the following order of priority:

1. Wages, severance allowances, social insurance, health insurance, unemployment insurance, and other entitlements owed to employees under signed labour contracts. [2]
2. Payments by insurance and reinsurance enterprises of claims, insurance proceeds, surrender values, contract account values, or premium refunds that have been approved for payment. [2]
3. Tax debts. [2]
4. Other debts. [2]

Following settlement of all debts in accordance with this priority order, the insurance, reinsurance, or insurance brokerage enterprise proceeds with dissolution in accordance with the guidance of the Managing Authority (Cơ quan điều hành). [2]

This debt priority framework has direct implications for the recovery of capital by creditors and investors in the event that a TTTC-based financial institution is wound up.
