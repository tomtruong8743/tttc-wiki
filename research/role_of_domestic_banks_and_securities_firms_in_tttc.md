# Role of Domestic Banks and Securities Firms in TTTC

## Summary

Domestic banks and securities firms are anticipated participants in the [[Tttc Financial Center Overview|Vietnam International Financial Centre (TTTC)]], operating alongside foreign institutions within the special regulatory framework established by the Vietnamese Government. Their roles are governed by the suite of decrees issued by the [[Legal Framework Of Tttc|Government Office (Văn Phòng Chính Phủ)]], including [[Decree 323 Establishment Of Tttc]], [[Decree 324 Financial Policies Of Tttc]], [[Decree 329 Banking And Foreign Exchange]], and [[Decree 330 Commodities Exchange In Tttc]], which collectively define the legal and financial environment in which all market participants — domestic and foreign — may operate. Notably, [[Decree 329 Banking And Foreign Exchange]] contains specific provisions distinguishing between domestic-capital and foreign-capital members, particularly regarding cross-border lending activities and [[Foreign Exchange Rules In The Vietnam International Financial Centre|foreign exchange management]].

## Key Points

- The source documents were all issued by the Government Office of Vietnam (Văn Phòng Chính Phủ), indicating that the regulatory framework governing domestic banks and securities firms in the [[Tttc Financial Center Overview|TTTC]] originates at the highest level of government. [1][2][3][4][5][6]
- [[Decree 323 Establishment Of Tttc]] provides the foundational legal basis for the establishment of the TTTC, within which domestic financial institutions would be expected to operate. [5]
- [[Decree 324 Financial Policies Of Tttc]] covers financial policies applicable within the TTTC, which would be relevant to the operations of domestic banks and securities firms. [1]
- Under [[Decree 329 Banking And Foreign Exchange]], members that are **not** 100% foreign-investor-owned — including domestically capitalised banks — are subject to specific conditions when lending abroad (cho vay ra nước ngoài), as set out in Article 89 of that decree. [6]
- A 100% domestically capitalised commercial bank (ngân hàng thương mại 100% vốn trong nước) that is a TTTC member is permitted to lend in foreign currency to: (a) organisations established and operating outside Vietnam in which Vietnamese enterprises hold more than 50% of shares or contributed capital; and (b) organisations held more than 50% by such qualifying entities. [6]
- A member enterprise (doanh nghiệp thành viên) that is not 100% foreign-owned may lend to an overseas organisation in which the lender itself or its parent company holds more than 50% of shares or contributed capital. [6]
- All TTTC members — including domestic institutions — that borrow in foreign currency from individuals or organisations outside Vietnam must register and comply with reporting obligations (khai báo thông tin, tuân thủ chế độ báo cáo). [6]
- Members that are 100% foreign-investor-owned enjoy broader outbound lending rights than domestically capitalised members, reflecting a deliberate policy distinction within the [[Ngoai Hoi In The Context Of Tttc|foreign exchange management]] framework. [6]
- [[Decree 329 Banking And Foreign Exchange]] references credit ratings from Standard & Poor's, Moody's, or Fitch Ratings as relevant criteria within the banking licensing and establishment framework, suggesting that domestic institutions seeking certain activities may need to meet internationally recognised rating standards. [1]
- [[Decree 330 Commodities Exchange In Tttc]] establishes a licensing and approval framework for the [[Decree 330 Commodities Exchange In Tttc|Commodities Exchange (Sở giao dịch hàng hóa)]] within the TTTC, including approval for a Clearing Centre (Trung tâm thanh toán bù trừ). Domestic banks and securities firms with clearing or settlement capabilities may be relevant participants in this structure. [3][4]
- The broader legal framework includes provisions on land and environment ([[Decree 326 Land And Environment In Tttc]]), labour and social security ([[Decree 325 Labor And Social Security In Tttc]]), immigration ([[Decree 327 Immigration Policy]]), and international arbitration ([[Decree 328 International Arbitration Center]]), all of which affect how domestic institutions may establish and run operations within the TTTC. [3][6][2][4]

## Definitions

- [[Tttc Financial Center Overview|TTTC]] — The Vietnam International Financial Centre, a special financial zone established by the Vietnamese Government under a series of decrees. [5]
- [[Decree 324 Financial Policies Of Tttc|Financial policies]] — Regulations governing financial activities within the TTTC, issued under Decree 324 by the Government Office. [1]
- [[Legal Framework Of Tttc|Văn Phòng Chính Phủ]] — The Government Office of Vietnam; the issuing authority for all the foundational TTTC decrees. [1][2][3][4][5][6]
- **Ngân hàng thương mại 100% vốn trong nước** — A 100% domestically capitalised commercial bank; a category of TTTC member subject to specific cross-border lending conditions under Article 89 of [[Decree 329 Banking And Foreign Exchange]]. [6]
- **Thành viên** — "Member"; the term used in [[Decree 329 Banking And Foreign Exchange]] to refer to participating financial institutions in the TTTC, including both domestic and foreign-owned entities. [6]
- **Cho vay ra nước ngoài** — Outbound/cross-border lending; an activity regulated for domestically capitalised TTTC members under Article 89 of [[Decree 329 Banking And Foreign Exchange]]. [6]
- **Trung tâm thanh toán bù trừ** — Clearing Centre; an entity approved under [[Decree 330 Commodities Exchange In Tttc]] to perform clearing and settlement functions within the TTTC Commodities Exchange. [3][4]
- [[Ngoai Hoi In The Context Of Tttc|Ngoại hối]] — Foreign exchange; regulated under Section 4 of [[Decree 329 Banking And Foreign Exchange]], which sets out principles for borrowing and lending in foreign currency by TTTC members. [6]

## Outbound Lending Rules for Domestic Members

Under Article 89 of [[Decree 329 Banking And Foreign Exchange]], a domestically capitalised TTTC member (i.e., not 100% foreign-investor-owned) must satisfy specific conditions before lending to overseas entities. These rules distinguish between two categories of eligible borrowers:

1. **Overseas organisations with majority Vietnamese ownership** — Organisations established and operating outside Vietnam in which a Vietnamese enterprise (whether or not a TTTC member) holds more than 50% of shares or contributed capital. [6]
2. **Subsidiaries of qualifying overseas organisations** — Organisations that are themselves more than 50% held by the entities described in category (1). [6]
3. **Member enterprises (doanh nghiệp thành viên)** — May lend to overseas organisations in which they or their parent company hold more than 50% of shares or contributed capital. [6]

These rules reflect the [[Foreign Exchange Rules In The Vietnam International Financial Centre|foreign exchange management]] policy of the TTTC, which grants broader outbound lending freedoms to fully foreign-owned members while imposing structured conditions on domestically capitalised entities. All outbound lending by domestic members requires information registration and compliance with reporting obligations. [6]

This framework is relevant to [[Cross Border Ma In The Vietnam International Financial Centre|cross-border M&A]] and [[International Capital Flows In The Vietnam International Financial Centre|international capital flow]] activities conducted through the TTTC, and has implications for how [[Domestic Banks In The Vietnam International Financial Centre|domestic banks]] structure their international operations via the centre.
