# Corporate Income Tax Incentives in the Vietnam International Financial Centre

## Summary

The Vietnam International Financial Centre (TTTC) offers a range of financial policy incentives to attract investment and financial activity, governed by decrees issued by the Vietnamese Government Office. [[Decree 324 Financial Policies Of Tttc]] is the primary legal instrument addressing financial policies within the TTTC, including provisions related to [[Tax Incentives And Exemptions]] applicable to entities operating within the centre. These incentives form a core part of the broader [[Legal Framework Of Tttc]] designed to position the TTTC as a competitive international financial hub. Complementary rules on banking, foreign exchange, and accounting standards — found in [[Decree 329 Banking And Foreign Exchange]] — further shape the operational and financial environment in which corporate income tax incentives apply.

## Key Points

- [[Decree 324 Financial Policies Of Tttc]] was issued by the Government Office (Văn Phòng Chính Phủ) and governs financial policies within the TTTC, which would include corporate income tax incentive arrangements [1].
- The TTTC's financial policy framework is established alongside complementary decrees covering land and environment ([[Decree 326 Land And Environment In Tttc]]), labour and social security ([[Decree 325 Labor And Social Security In Tttc]]), immigration ([[Decree 327 Immigration Policy]]), banking and foreign exchange ([[Decree 329 Banking And Foreign Exchange]]), and commodities exchange ([[Decree 330 Commodities Exchange In Tttc]]), suggesting a comprehensive incentive package for participants [1][3][6][2].
- All foundational decrees, including those governing financial policies, were issued by the same authority — the Vietnamese Government Office (Văn Phòng Chính Phủ) — indicating centralised policy coordination [1][2][3][4][5][6].
- The establishment of the TTTC itself is governed by [[Decree 323 Establishment Of Tttc]], which provides the legal basis upon which financial incentives in [[Decree 324 Financial Policies Of Tttc]] operate [5][1].
- Members (Thành viên) of the TTTC are permitted to apply international accounting standards (**Chuẩn mực quốc tế về kế toán**) or generally accepted accounting principles (GAAP) when preparing financial statements and consolidated financial statements, and where they do so, they are not required to additionally prepare statements under Vietnamese Accounting Standards (**Chuẩn mực kế toán Việt Nam**) [6]. This accounting flexibility is directly relevant to how corporate income is measured and reported for tax purposes.
- Financial statements prepared under international accounting standards or generally accepted accounting principles constitute the legal financial reports of the member entity, to be submitted to competent authorities and publicly disclosed in accordance with applicable law [6].
- Members must submit financial statements and consolidated financial statements to the Governing Authority (Cơ quan điều hành), the Supervisory Authority (Cơ quan giám sát), and other competent Vietnamese authorities, with reporting periods and deadlines governed by accounting law [6].
- Members bear legal responsibility for their choice to apply international accounting standards or generally accepted accounting principles when preparing financial statements [6].
- Under [[Decree 329 Banking And Foreign Exchange]], foreign investors investing into the TTTC must channel funds through a foreign investor capital account opened at a member bank, underscoring the link between [[Foreign Exchange Rules In The Vietnam International Financial Centre]] and the broader tax and financial incentive framework [3].
- 100% foreign-owned member banks and foreign bank branches operating within the TTTC are permitted to conduct a wide range of activities with organisations on Vietnamese territory, including lending, discounting, bank guarantees, factoring, letters of credit, and buying and selling corporate bonds and government bonds — activities whose profitability would fall within the scope of any applicable [[Corporate Income Tax Incentives In The Vietnam International Financial Centre]] [4].
- Members that are 100% foreign-owned are permitted to lend to individuals and organisations outside Vietnamese territory, subject to reporting obligations, reflecting the cross-border nature of TTTC operations and their tax implications [5].

## Definitions

- [[Decree 324 Financial Policies Of Tttc]] — The decree issued by the Vietnamese Government Office establishing financial policies within the TTTC, including provisions that are expected to cover corporate income tax incentives [1].
- [[Tttc Financial Center Overview]] — The Vietnam International Financial Centre (Trung Tâm Tài Chính — TTTC), established under [[Decree 323 Establishment Of Tttc]] as a special financial zone subject to dedicated legal and tax frameworks [5].
- **Văn Phòng Chính Phủ** — The Government Office of Vietnam, the issuing authority for all foundational TTTC decrees [1][2][3][4][5][6].
- **Chuẩn mực quốc tế về kế toán** — International accounting standards (IFRS or equivalent) that TTTC members may elect to apply for financial reporting, replacing the requirement to use Vietnamese Accounting Standards [6].
- **Chuẩn mực kế toán Việt Nam** — Vietnamese Accounting Standards; TTTC members who adopt international standards are exempt from the obligation to additionally prepare reports under these domestic standards [6].
- **Thành viên** — "Member"; an entity admitted to and operating within the TTTC, subject to its financial, tax, and regulatory framework [6].
- **Cơ quan điều hành** — The Governing Authority of the TTTC, one of the competent bodies to which financial statements must be submitted [6].
- **Cơ quan giám sát** — The Supervisory Authority of the TTTC, another body to which financial statements must be submitted [6]. See also [[Supervision Mechanisms In The Vietnam International Financial Centre]].

## Accounting Standards and Tax Reporting

A notable feature of the TTTC's financial policy framework is the flexibility granted to members regarding accounting standards. Under [[Decree 324 Financial Policies Of Tttc]], members may prepare financial statements and consolidated financial statements in accordance with international accounting standards or generally accepted accounting principles rather than Vietnamese Accounting Standards [6]. Key implications include:

- **Legal standing of international-standard reports**: Financial statements prepared under international standards are treated as the legally valid financial reports of the member entity for all purposes, including submission to regulators and public disclosure [6].
- **No dual reporting obligation**: Where a member elects to apply international standards, it is not required to also produce a parallel set of Vietnamese Accounting Standard reports [6].
- **Member responsibility**: The member entity bears full legal responsibility for its choice of accounting standard and for the accuracy of the resulting financial statements [6].
- **Relevance to corporate income tax**: Because taxable income is typically derived from audited financial statements, the choice of accounting standard may affect how income, expenses, and profits are calculated and reported for [[Corporate Income Tax Incentives In The Vietnam International Financial Centre]] purposes (uncertain — explicit linkage to tax calculations not confirmed in available sources).

This accounting flexibility aligns the TTTC with international financial centre norms and is likely intended to reduce compliance burdens for [[Foreign Banks In The Vietnam International Financial Centre]] and [[Asset Managers In The Vietnam International Financial Centre]] accustomed to IFRS or US GAAP reporting.
