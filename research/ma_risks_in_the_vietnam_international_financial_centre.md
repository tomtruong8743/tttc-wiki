# M&A Risks in the Vietnam International Financial Centre

## Summary

The Vietnam International Financial Centre (TTTC) operates under a framework established by a series of government decrees issued by the Vietnamese Government Office, covering areas such as financial policy, land and environment, immigration, international arbitration, labour, and establishment. For parties considering [[Mergers And Acquisitions In The Vietnam International Financial Centre]], understanding the risks associated with this regulatory environment is essential. Key risk areas may intersect with [[Legal And Financial Policies Affecting Ma In Tttc]], [[Foreign Ownership Rules In The Vietnam International Financial Centre]], and the [[Dispute Resolution Mechanisms]] available within the TTTC framework.

## Key Points

- The TTTC was established under a decree issued by the Vietnamese Government Office, forming the foundational legal basis that M&A participants must navigate. [5]
- Financial policies governing the TTTC are set out in a dedicated decree, which M&A parties must assess when evaluating deal structures and [[Tax Incentives And Exemptions]]. [1]
- Land and environment regulations applicable within the TTTC are governed by a separate decree, creating potential risks around asset ownership and property rights in M&A transactions. [3]
- Immigration policy within the TTTC is covered by its own decree, which may affect the ability to retain or transfer key personnel following an M&A transaction. [2]
- An international arbitration centre forms part of the TTTC framework, providing a mechanism for resolving post-M&A disputes. [4]
- Labour, employment, and social security matters within the TTTC are governed by a dedicated decree, creating obligations relevant to workforce integration in M&A deals. [6]
