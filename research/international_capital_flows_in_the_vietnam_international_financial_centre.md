# International Capital Flows in the Vietnam International Financial Centre

## Summary

International capital flows are a central component of the [[What Is The Vietnam International Financial Centre|Vietnam International Financial Centre (TTTC)]], which was established through a series of government decrees to facilitate the movement of capital across borders within a specialised financial zone. The framework governing these flows is set out across multiple decrees issued by the [[Legal Documents Establishing The Vietnam International Financial Centre|Vietnamese Government Office]], including [[Decree 323 Establishment Of Tttc]], [[Decree 324 Financial Policies Of Tttc]], and [[Decree 329 Banking And Foreign Exchange]], among others. These instruments together define how [[Foreign Exchange Rules In The Vietnam International Financial Centre|foreign exchange]], [[Capital Movement Rules In The Vietnam International Financial Centre|capital movement]], and related financial activities are regulated within the TTTC.

[[Decree 329 Banking And Foreign Exchange]] contains particularly detailed provisions on the mechanics of cross-border capital flows, including rules on borrowing from offshore parties, lending abroad, [[Foreign Currency Transactions In The Vietnam International Financial Centre|foreign currency transactions]], and the specific activities permitted for different categories of member banks.

## Key Points

- The TTTC framework is established through a suite of decrees issued by the Vietnamese Government Office (VĂN PHÒNG CHÍNH PHỦ), each addressing a distinct aspect of the centre's operations. [1][2][3][4][5][6]
- [[Decree 323 Establishment Of Tttc]] provides the foundational legal basis for the TTTC, within which [[Capital Movement Rules In The Vietnam International Financial Centre|international capital flow rules]] operate. [5]
- [[Decree 324 Financial Policies Of Tttc]] covers the [[Tax Incentives And Exemptions|financial policies]] that govern activities within the TTTC, which are directly relevant to the treatment of [[Transferring Capital Into And Out Of The Vietnam International Financial Centre|inbound and outbound capital]]. [1]
- [[Decree 326 Land And Environment In Tttc]] regulates land and environmental matters within the TTTC, including a requirement that TTTC members register environmental compliance for investment projects before discharging waste, which forms part of the broader investment environment affecting [[Foreign Investment Rules In Tttc|foreign capital inflows]]. [1][3]
- [[Decree 327 Immigration Policy]] addresses immigration and entry/exit policies, which support the movement of foreign financial professionals and investors connected to capital flow activities. [2]
- [[Decree 328 International Arbitration Center]] establishes an international arbitration centre within the TTTC, providing [[Legal Protections For Investors In The Vietnam International Financial Centre|dispute resolution mechanisms]] that underpin investor confidence in cross-border capital flows. [4]
- [[Decree 325 Labor And Social Security In Tttc]] governs labour, employment, and social security within the TTTC, forming part of the operating environment for institutions involved in [[Capital Markets In The Vietnam International Financial Centre|capital market activities]]. [6]
- All decrees were issued by the same authority — the Vietnamese Government Office — indicating a coordinated, whole-of-government approach to regulating the TTTC and its capital flow framework. [1][2][3][4][5][6]

### Cross-Border Borrowing and Lending Rules

[[Decree 329 Banking And Foreign Exchange]] sets out detailed rules on the management of foreign exchange for borrowing and lending activities involving TTTC members, including:

- **Offshore borrowing:** TTTC members are permitted to borrow in foreign currency from individuals and organisations outside Vietnamese territory, subject to mandatory information disclosure and reporting obligations. [4]
- **Offshore lending — 100% foreign-owned members:** Members whose charter capital is 100% owned by foreign investors are permitted to lend in foreign currency to individuals and organisations outside Vietnamese territory, again subject to disclosure and reporting requirements. [4]
- **Offshore lending — non-100% foreign-owned members:** Members not wholly owned by foreign investors must comply with additional conditions for cross-border lending as specified in [[Decree 329 Banking And Foreign Exchange]], including restrictions tied to the borrower's ownership structure (e.g., organisations outside Vietnam in which Vietnamese enterprises hold more than 50% of shares or contributed capital). [4]
- **Domestic banks lending abroad in foreign currency:** 100% domestically owned commercial banks that are TTTC members may lend in foreign currency to organisations established and operating outside Vietnam that are majority-owned (over 50%) by Vietnamese enterprises that are not themselves members, or to organisations majority-owned by such qualifying entities. [4]
- **Member enterprise lending abroad:** Member enterprises may lend to organisations established outside Vietnam in which the lending member or its parent company holds more than 50% of shares or contributed capital. [4]

These provisions represent a significant area of [[Ngoai Hoi In The Context Of Tttc|ngoại hối (foreign exchange)]] regulation specifically tailored to the TTTC's cross-border capital flow objectives.

### Permitted Activities for Foreign Bank Members

Under [[Decree 329 Banking And Foreign Exchange]], 100% foreign-owned commercial banks and foreign bank branches that are TTTC members are authorised to conduct a range of activities with non-credit-institution organisations on Vietnamese territory, including:

- Lending, discounting, and bank guarantees. [2]
- Factoring and related services. [2]
- Letter of credit (L/C) operations and related business activities. [2]
- Buying and selling corporate bonds issued on Vietnamese territory (excluding bonds issued by Vietnamese credit institutions). [2]
- Buying and selling Vietnamese Government bonds, government-guaranteed bonds, and local government bonds, including purchases on the primary market. [2]
- Debt purchase ([[Debt Capital Markets In The Vietnam International Financial Centre|mua nợ]]). [2]

These permissions directly support [[How Companies Raise Capital Through The Vietnam International Financial Centre|capital raising]] by Vietnamese companies and [[Bond Issuance Opportunities In The Vietnam International Financial Centre|bond market participation]] by foreign institutions.

### Permitted Activities for Domestic Bank Members

100% domestically owned commercial banks that are TTTC members may conduct activities with Vietnamese credit institutions in specific circumstances, including:

- Receiving bank guarantees from Vietnamese credit institutions where the guarantee beneficiary is a Vietnamese individual or organisation, or where the domestic bank deposits 100% of the guarantee value. [5]
- Providing counter-guarantees to Vietnamese credit institutions where the credit institution issues a guarantee for a member enterprise that is a customer of the domestic bank, subject to similar conditions. [5]
- Having guarantees confirmed by Vietnamese credit institutions under defined conditions. [5]
- Factoring and letter of credit services. [5]
- Opening payment accounts in Vietnamese dong or foreign currency at non-member commercial banks and foreign bank branches in accordance with applicable law. [5]

### Environmental Registration for Investment Projects

Under [[Decree 326 Land And Environment In Tttc]], TTTC members that are investment project sponsors must register environmental compliance before discharging waste. Registration is submitted to the commune-level People's Committee (Ủy ban nhân dân cấp xã) where the project is located, or — notably — through the [[Facilities And Zones In The Vietnam International Financial Centre|TTTC Operating Authority]] (Cơ quan điều hành Trung tâm tài chính quốc tế) in [[Ho Chi Minh City Vs Da Nang In The Vietnam International Financial Centre|Ho Chi Minh City or Da Nang]]. Where a project spans two or more communes, the project sponsor may choose which commune-level authority to register with. [1][3]

This environmental registration requirement, while not directly a capital flow mechanism, forms part of the regulatory compliance environment for [[Foreign Investment Rules In Tttc|foreign and domestic investment projects]] within the TTTC.

### Governance and Ethics Standards for Bank Officials

[[Decree 329 Banking And Foreign Exchange]] establishes professional ethics and governance standards for key personnel at member banks operating within the TTTC. Individuals who bear responsibility for findings that led to administrative penalties in the monetary and banking sector — particularly in areas such as licensing, governance, shareholding, credit extension, corporate bond purchases, or safety ratio compliance — are deemed not to meet professional ethics standards. [6] These governance requirements are relevant to [[Compliance Requirements In The Vietnam International Financial Centre|compliance]] and [[Risk Management Requirements In The Vietnam International Financial Centre|risk management]] for institutions involved in international capital flows.
