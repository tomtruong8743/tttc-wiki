# How the Vietnam International Financial Centre Attracts Foreign Capital

## Summary

The Vietnam International Financial Centre (TTTC) is established and governed through a suite of decrees issued by the Vietnamese Government Office ([[Decree 323 Establishment Of Tttc]]), with supporting regulations covering [[Tax Incentives And Exemptions]], immigration, land, labour, and dispute resolution. Together, these legal instruments form the framework through which the TTTC seeks to attract foreign capital by creating a specialised, internationally oriented financial environment. Related policies address [[Currency And Capital Controls]], [[Foreign Investment Rules In Tttc]], and [[Dispute Resolution Mechanisms]], all designed to make the centre competitive with established regional financial hubs. [[Decree 329 Banking And Foreign Exchange]] adds a further layer of attraction by defining specific banking permissions and foreign exchange management rules that enable cross-border lending, foreign currency transactions, and a broad range of banking activities for both foreign and domestically owned member institutions.

## Key Points

- The TTTC was formally established by a decree issued by the Vietnamese Government Office, providing the foundational [[Legal Framework Of Tttc]] upon which all foreign capital attraction mechanisms rest. [5-orig]
- [[Decree 324 Financial Policies Of Tttc]] sets out the financial policy framework within the centre, which is a core instrument for incentivising foreign investment. [1-orig]
- [[Decree 327 Immigration Policy]] governs entry and exit policies within the TTTC, suggesting that eased immigration conditions are part of the strategy to attract foreign financial professionals and capital. [2-orig]
- [[Decree 326 Land And Environment In Tttc]] addresses land and environmental regulations within the centre, providing a regulatory basis for foreign institutions seeking to establish a physical presence. [3-orig]
- [[Decree 328 International Arbitration Center]] establishes an international arbitration centre within the TTTC, indicating that [[Dispute Resolution Mechanisms]] are a deliberate feature to build foreign investor confidence. [4-orig]
- [[Decree 325 Labor And Social Security In Tttc]] covers labour and social security policies, which are relevant to [[Foreign Worker Regulations]] and the ability of foreign firms to staff operations within the centre. [6-orig]
- All foundational decrees were issued by the same authority — VĂN PHÒNG CHÍNH PHỦ (the Government Office of Vietnam) — reflecting a coordinated, top-down policy approach to foreign capital attraction. [1-orig][2-orig][3-orig][4-orig][5-orig][6-orig]
- [[Decree 329 Banking And Foreign Exchange]] governs licensing, establishment of banks, and [[Foreign Exchange Rules In The Vietnam International Financial Centre]] within the TTTC, providing a detailed operational framework for [[Foreign Banks In The Vietnam International Financial Centre]] and domestically owned member institutions. [1][2][4][5][6]
- Under Decree 329, TTTC members are permitted to borrow foreign currency from individuals and organisations outside Vietnamese territory, subject to reporting and disclosure requirements — a key feature enabling [[International Capital Flows In The Vietnam International Financial Centre]]. [1]
- Members that are 100% foreign-investor-owned are permitted to lend to individuals and organisations outside Vietnamese territory, subject to reporting requirements, while members that are not 100% foreign-owned must satisfy additional conditions set out in the decree. [1]
- Domestically owned member banks (100% domestic capital) may lend in foreign currency to overseas organisations in which a Vietnamese enterprise holds more than 50% of shares or contributed capital, as well as to organisations controlled by such entities — facilitating Vietnamese companies' international expansion via the TTTC. [1]
- 100% foreign-owned commercial banks and foreign bank branches that are TTTC members may conduct a wide range of activities with non-credit-institution organisations on Vietnamese territory, including lending, discounting, bank guarantees, factoring, letters of credit, buying and selling corporate bonds issued in Vietnam (excluding bonds issued by Vietnamese credit institutions), and buying and selling Vietnamese Government bonds (including on the primary market). [2]
- Foreign bank members are also permitted to buy and sell debt, conduct trust activities, provide banking advisory services, offer treasury and asset safekeeping services, and rent safety deposit boxes, subject to the scope of their licence issued by the city-level management authority. [6]
- Domestic 100% Vietnamese-capital commercial banks operating as TTTC members may provide bank guarantees, counter-guarantees, and confirmed guarantees, subject to conditions including full 100% collateral deposit or guarantee by a Vietnamese credit institution. [4]
- Both foreign and domestic member banks may open payment accounts in Vietnamese dong and foreign currency at non-member commercial banks and foreign bank branches, and may conduct foreign currency transactions in accordance with State Bank of Vietnam regulations. [4][5]
- The infrastructure registration process under [[Decree 324 Financial Policies Of Tttc]] includes a facility description form requiring organisations to document office space, equipment, software systems, crowdfunding platform infrastructure, and customer asset management systems — indicating that physical and technical readiness is a formal prerequisite for participation. [3]

## Definitions

- [[Tttc Financial Center Overview|TTTC]] — Vietnam International Financial Centre (Trung tâm Tài chính Quốc tế), the specialised financial zone established by the Vietnamese government to attract foreign capital and financial institutions. [5-orig]
- [[Decree 323 Establishment Of Tttc|Decree 323]] — The foundational decree establishing the TTTC, issued by the Vietnamese Government Office. [5-orig]
- [[Decree 324 Financial Policies Of Tttc|Decree 324]] — The decree governing financial policies within the TTTC, a key instrument for defining incentives relevant to foreign capital. [1-orig]
- [[Decree 329 Banking And Foreign Exchange|Decree 329]] — The decree governing banking licensing, establishment, and [[Ngoai Hoi In The Context Of Tttc|foreign exchange management]] (quản lý ngoại hối) within the TTTC, including rules on cross-border lending and permitted activities for member banks. [1][2][4][5][6]
- [[Decree 328 International Arbitration Center|International Arbitration Centre]] — A dedicated dispute resolution body established within the TTTC, intended to provide foreign investors with internationally recognised legal recourse. [4-orig]
- **Thành viên (Member)** — An institution or enterprise that has been admitted as a member of the TTTC and is thereby subject to — and entitled to benefit from — the special regulatory framework established by the TTTC decrees, including the banking and foreign exchange rules under Decree 329. [1][2][4][5][6]
- **Ngân hàng thương mại 100% vốn nước ngoài** — A 100% foreign-capital commercial bank; a category of member bank permitted to carry out an extensive range of activities within the TTTC under Decree 329. [2][5][6]
- **Ngân hàng thương mại 100% vốn trong nước** — A 100% domestically capitalised commercial bank; subject to additional conditions when conducting cross-border lending or guarantee activities under Decree 329. [1][4]
- **VĂN PHÒNG CHÍNH PHỦ** — The Government Office of Vietnam; the issuing authority for all TTTC-related decrees covered in these sources. [1-orig][2-orig][3-orig][4-orig][5-orig][6-orig]

## Foreign Exchange and Cross-Border Capital Rules

[[Decree 329 Banking And Foreign Exchange]] contains a dedicated section on foreign exchange management for borrowing and lending activities of TTTC members. Key principles include:

- **Cross-border borrowing:** All TTTC members may borrow foreign currency from individuals and organisations outside Vietnamese territory, subject to information disclosure and reporting obligations. This rule applies regardless of the ownership structure of the member. [1]
- **Cross-border lending — 100% foreign-owned members:** Members wholly owned by foreign investors may lend to individuals and organisations outside Vietnamese territory, again subject to disclosure and reporting requirements. [1]
- **Cross-border lending — mixed or domestic ownership:** Members not wholly owned by foreign investors must meet additional eligibility conditions set out in Article 89 of the decree before lending abroad. Specifically, 100% domestically capitalised commercial banks may lend in foreign currency to: (a) overseas organisations in which Vietnamese enterprises hold more than 50% of shares or contributed capital; and (b) overseas organisations controlled by such entities. [1]
- **Member enterprise lending:** Member enterprises (non-bank members) may lend to overseas organisations in which the lending member or its parent company holds more than 50% of shares or contributed capital. [1]
