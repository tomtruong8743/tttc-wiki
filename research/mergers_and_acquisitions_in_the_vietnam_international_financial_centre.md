# Mergers and Acquisitions in the Vietnam International Financial Centre

## Summary

Mergers and acquisitions (M&A) activity within the [[Vietnam International Financial Centre]] (TTTC) is shaped by the broader legal and financial framework established by the five foundational decrees. While the source documents do not contain a dedicated chapter or article explicitly addressing M&A transactions, several provisions across [[Decree 329 Banking And Foreign Exchange]] and [[Decree 324 Financial Policies Of Tttc]] are directly relevant to M&A structuring, financing, and advisory work within the TTTC. These include rules on debt purchase and sale, bond transactions, cross-border lending, foreign ownership of capital, and advisory services provided by member banks.

M&A practitioners and [[investment banking opportunities in the Vietnam International Financial Centre|investment banks]] operating in the TTTC should treat these provisions as the primary regulatory touchpoints for deal structuring until more specific M&A regulations are issued.

---

## Key Points

### Debt Purchase and Sale (Mua, Bán Nợ)
- Under [[Decree 329 Banking And Foreign Exchange]], both 100% foreign-owned commercial banks and foreign bank branches that are TTTC members are explicitly permitted to **buy and sell debt** (*mua, bán nợ*). [2][3][6]
- Domestically owned commercial banks that are TTTC members are similarly permitted to buy debt (*mua nợ*) from organisations on Vietnamese territory. [6]
- Debt purchase and sale is a core M&A-adjacent activity, as it underpins distressed asset acquisitions, loan portfolio transfers, and leveraged buyout financing structures.

### Bond Transactions Relevant to M&A Financing
- Member banks — both foreign and domestic — are permitted to buy and sell:
  - Vietnamese corporate bonds issued on Vietnamese territory (excluding bonds issued by Vietnamese credit institutions) [2][3][6]
  - Vietnamese Government bonds, Government-guaranteed bonds, and local government bonds [2][3][6]
- Foreign-owned member banks may purchase Government bonds on the primary market. [3]
- Foreign-owned member banks are **prohibited** from selling Vietnamese Government bonds, Government-guaranteed bonds, or local government bonds **denominated in foreign currency**. [3][6]
- These bond permissions are significant for M&A because they allow TTTC member banks to arrange and hold acquisition financing instruments and bridge bonds. See also [[Debt Capital Markets In The Vietnam International Financial Centre]] and [[Bond Issuance Opportunities In The Vietnam International Financial Centre]].

### Advisory Services
- 100% foreign-owned commercial banks and foreign bank branches that are TTTC members may provide **advisory services** (*dịch vụ tư vấn*) on banking activities and other compatible business activities, within the scope of their licence issued by the TTTC city-level governing authority. [2]
- Advisory solutions and recommendations must be executed by the member bank itself or another suitable member bank within the scope of its licence. [2]
- This provision supports M&A advisory mandates, including sell-side and buy-side advisory, financing advisory, and restructuring advisory. See [[Corporate Advisory Opportunities In The Vietnam International Financial Centre]].

### Cross-Border Lending Relevant to M&A Structures
- TTTC members may borrow in foreign currency from individuals and organisations outside Vietnamese territory, subject to information disclosure and reporting requirements. [4]
- Members that are 100% foreign-investor-owned may lend in foreign currency to individuals and organisations outside Vietnamese territory, subject to reporting obligations. [4]
- Members **not** 100% foreign-investor-owned (including 100% domestically owned commercial banks) may lend abroad in foreign currency only to:
  - Organisations established and operating outside Vietnam in which a Vietnamese enterprise (that is not itself a TTTC member) holds more than 50% of shares or contributed capital; or
  - Organisations established and operating outside Vietnam that are in turn more than 50% owned by the above-described entities. [4]
- Member enterprises (non-bank) may lend to organisations established outside Vietnam in which the lending member or its parent company holds more than 50% of shares or contributed capital. [4]
- These cross-border lending rules are central to financing outbound M&A by Vietnamese groups and inbound acquisitions by foreign investors. See [[Cross Border Ma In The Vietnam International Financial Centre]] and [[Capital Movement Rules In The Vietnam International Financial Centre]].

### Infrastructure for Capital Raising (Crowdfunding Platforms)
- [[Decree 324 Financial Policies Of Tttc]] includes a standardised disclosure form (*Bản thuyết minh cơ sở vật chất*) for organisations seeking registration, which requires disclosure of physical premises, office equipment, and systems, including systems serving **crowdfunding capital-raising platforms** (*nền tảng huy động vốn cộng đồng*) and **client asset management activities**. [1]
- This suggests the TTTC framework contemplates equity and quasi-equity capital-raising mechanisms that may be relevant to early-stage M&A and growth capital transactions. See [[Capital Raising In The Vietnam International Financial Centre]] and [[How Companies Raise Capital Through The Vietnam International Financial Centre]].

### Professional Conduct Standards Affecting M&A Advisers
- [[Decree 329 Banking And Foreign Exchange]] sets out professional ethics (*đạo đức nghề nghiệp*) standards for senior officers of member banks, including disqualifying criteria based on prior regulatory sanctions related to licences, governance, shareholding, capital contribution, share purchases (*mua cổ phần*), credit extension, and corporate bond purchases. [5]
- These conduct standards affect who may serve as a director, supervisor, or chief accountant of a TTTC member bank, and therefore indirectly affect the governance due diligence required in any acquisition of a TTTC-licensed entity. See [[Legal Due Diligence Questions For The Vietnam International Financial Centre]] and [[Compliance Due Diligence Questions For The Vietnam International Financial Centre]].

## Gaps and Limitations

The current source documents do not contain explicit provisions addressing:
- Mandatory or voluntary takeover thresholds
- Foreign ownership caps for M&A in specific sectors within the TTTC
- Pre-merger notification or competition clearance procedures
- Squeeze-out or minority buyout mechanisms
- Special Purpose Vehicles (SPVs) for M&A structuring within the TTTC

These matters may be governed by Vietnamese national law (e.g., the Law on Enterprises, the Law on Investment, the Law on Securities) as applied within or alongside the TTTC framework, or may be addressed in implementing regulations not yet available. See [[Information Missing From Tttc Source Documents]] and [[Legal And Financial Policies Affecting Ma In Tttc]].
