# Debt Capital Markets in the Vietnam International Financial Centre

## Summary

Debt Capital Markets (DCM) within the [[What Is The Vietnam International Financial Centre|Vietnam International Financial Centre (TTTC)]] refers to the frameworks, instruments, and activities related to the issuance and trading of debt securities within the centre. The TTTC is governed by a suite of foundational decrees issued by the Vietnamese Government Office ([[Decree 323 Establishment Of Tttc]], [[Decree 324 Financial Policies Of Tttc]], and related instruments), which collectively establish the legal, financial, and operational environment in which debt capital market activities may take place. The specific contours of DCM activity within the TTTC are shaped by policies covering [[Tax Incentives And Exemptions]], [[Currency And Capital Controls]], [[Foreign Investment Rules In Tttc]], and [[Dispute Resolution Mechanisms]].

[[Decree 329 Banking And Foreign Exchange]] provides detailed rules that are directly relevant to DCM participants, including provisions governing foreign currency borrowing and lending by TTTC member institutions, bond trading permissions for foreign bank branches and wholly foreign-owned commercial banks, and prudential safety ratios applicable to member banks — including specific exemptions from certain Vietnamese domestic requirements for banks applying international accounting standards.

---

## Key Points

- The TTTC is established and governed through a series of decrees issued by the Vietnamese Government Office (VĂN PHÒNG CHÍNH PHỦ), which form the foundational [[Legal Framework Of Tttc]] for all financial market activities, including debt capital markets. [1][2][3][4][5][6]
- [[Decree 323 Establishment Of Tttc]] provides the overarching establishment framework within which debt capital market participants and instruments would operate. [5]
- [[Decree 324 Financial Policies Of Tttc]] is the primary decree governing financial policies within the TTTC, and is expected to be the most directly relevant instrument for debt capital market regulation. [3]
- [[Decree 329 Banking And Foreign Exchange]] contains substantive provisions directly governing DCM-relevant activities, including foreign currency borrowing and lending, bond trading permissions, and prudential requirements for member banks. [1][2][5]
- [[Decree 326 Land And Environment In Tttc]] and [[Decree 325 Labor And Social Security In Tttc]] address ancillary operational conditions within the TTTC that may affect the operational environment for debt market participants. [prior sources]
- [[Decree 327 Immigration Policy]] governs entry and exit policies relevant to foreign professionals and investors participating in TTTC debt markets. [prior sources]
- [[Decree 328 International Arbitration Center]] establishes [[Dispute Resolution In The Vietnam International Financial Centre|dispute resolution]] infrastructure that would support the enforceability of debt instruments and contracts within the TTTC. [prior sources]
- All decrees are issued by the same issuing authority — the Vietnamese Government Office — indicating a coordinated, government-level approach to establishing the TTTC's financial ecosystem. [prior sources]

## Foreign Currency Borrowing and Lending

[[Decree 329 Banking And Foreign Exchange]] (Section 4, Articles 87–89) sets out specific [[Foreign Exchange Rules In The Vietnam International Financial Centre|foreign exchange]] rules governing borrowing and lending activities by TTTC member institutions:

### Borrowing from Overseas (Article 87)
- TTTC members are permitted to borrow foreign currency from individuals and organisations outside Vietnamese territory.
- Members must declare the relevant information and comply with applicable reporting requirements. [1]

### Lending Overseas (Article 88)
- TTTC members that are 100% foreign investor-owned may lend to individuals and organisations outside Vietnamese territory, subject to information declaration and reporting obligations. [1]
- TTTC members that are **not** 100% foreign investor-owned must comply with the additional conditions for overseas lending set out in Article 89, as well as information declaration and reporting obligations. [1]

### Conditions for Overseas Lending by Non-Wholly Foreign-Owned Members (Article 89)
- A 100% domestically-owned commercial bank member may lend in foreign currency to:
  - An organisation established and operating outside Vietnamese territory in which Vietnamese enterprises (whether members or non-members) hold more than 50% of shares or contributed capital; or
  - An organisation established and operating outside Vietnamese territory that is itself majority-owned (>50%) by such a qualifying organisation. [1]
- A member enterprise may lend to an organisation established and operating outside Vietnamese territory in which the lending member or its parent company holds more than 50% of shares or contributed capital. [1]

## Bond Trading Permissions for Foreign Banks

[[Decree 329 Banking And Foreign Exchange]] (Article 55) sets out the activities that 100% foreign-owned commercial banks and foreign bank branches operating as TTTC members may conduct with organisations on Vietnamese territory that are not Vietnamese credit institutions. These include several activities directly relevant to debt capital markets:

- **Buying and selling corporate bonds** issued on Vietnamese territory, excluding bonds issued by Vietnamese credit institutions. [2]
- **Buying and selling Vietnamese Government bonds**, Government-guaranteed bonds, and local government bonds in accordance with applicable law — including purchases on the primary market. [2]
- Loans, discounting, bank guarantees, factoring and related services, and letters of credit operations are also permitted, which may support DCM-adjacent financing and credit enhancement activities. [2]

## Prudential Requirements for Member Banks

[[Decree 329 Banking And Foreign Exchange]] (Articles 66–67) establishes prudential safety ratio requirements applicable to member banks, with certain modifications from standard Vietnamese domestic rules that are relevant to DCM participants:

- Member banks are subject to safety ratios including the **Leverage Ratio (LEV)**, **Liquidity Coverage Ratio (LCR)**, and **Net Stable Funding Ratio (NSFR)**. [5]
- The [[Financial Supervision And Governance|supervisory authority]] may require a member bank to apply stricter safety ratios than the standard thresholds, based on supervisory findings and the nature and level of risk identified. [5]
- **Banks applying international accounting standards are exempt** from the requirement to comply with Vietnamese law provisions governing the ratio for purchasing, holding, and investing in Vietnamese Government bonds and Government-guaranteed bonds. [5]
- Member banks are **not required to comply** with the maximum foreign currency position relative to equity capital under Vietnamese law. [5]
- In the event that a member bank's LCR falls below the required level, specific remediation procedures apply. [5]

These exemptions are significant for DCM participants, as they provide greater flexibility in managing government bond portfolios and foreign currency positions — key considerations for institutions active in debt securities markets.

## Guarantee and Bank Guarantee Activities

[[Decree 329 Banking And Foreign Exchange]] (Article 45 area) sets out conditions under which 100% domestically-owned commercial bank members may issue, receive, and confirm bank guarantees, including in relation to TTTC member enterprises. Relevant provisions include:

- A 100% domestically-owned commercial bank member may be issued a bank guarantee by a Vietnamese credit institution where the beneficiary is an individual or organisation on Vietnamese territory, or where the bank has deposited 100% of the guarantee value. [6]
- Counter-guarantee arrangements between 100% domestically-owned commercial banks and Vietnamese credit institutions are permitted in defined circumstances where the beneficiary is on Vietnamese territory. [6]
- Guarantee confirmation by Vietnamese credit institutions is permitted in defined circumstances. [6]

Bank guarantees are a key credit enhancement tool in debt capital markets, and these provisions define the scope of guarantee activity available to different categories of TTTC member banks.
